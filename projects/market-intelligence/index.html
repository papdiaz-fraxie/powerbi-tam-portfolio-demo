Business problem

A TAM number needed to become an actionable market plan.

An Australian telecommunications client needed a quantified view of companies matching its targeting requirements.

The original client-facing analysis identified 37,993 potentially addressable Australian companies, but the useful next question was where that opportunity sat and how campaign effort should be sequenced.

My response

Turn the targeting brief into measurable filters and prioritisation.

I translated geography, company-size, industry and other targeting requirements into practical data rules, then analysed the remaining market by state and other segments.

The goal was not simply to count companies. It was to show the trade-off between market coverage and targeting precision.

Method

From brief to usable market intelligence

A repeatable process makes a TAM defensible and easier to update.

1. Targeting briefDefine geography, company-size, industry and other ICP requirements.
2. Data filteringTranslate the brief into repeatable rules against the B2B dataset.
3. TAM sizingCount companies remaining after agreed criteria are applied.
4. SegmentationBreak the market down to expose concentration and trade-offs.
5. Commercial actionRecommend where campaign resources or further data work should focus.

Finding 1

NSW + Victoria = 63.7% of TAM.

Those two states alone contain 24,193 of the identified companies.

Finding 2

Adding Queensland lifts coverage to 80.9%.

That means the first three states contain more than four-fifths of the identified national opportunity.

Recommendation

Prioritise concentration first, then test whether tighter filters improve quality

A larger segment is not automatically a better segment once campaign outcomes are considered.

Start with coverage

NSW, Victoria and Queensland provide the most efficient first pass when broad national coverage matters.

Then narrow by ICP

Layer industry, employee-size, technology or other relevant signals within the concentrated states.

Measure the trade-off

Compare lost market coverage with whether tighter targeting actually produces stronger qualified outcomes.

Analytical judgement

Engagement is not the same as qualification

The public portfolio includes a synthetic post-campaign example to demonstrate this analytical principle.

In that demonstration, General Outbound produces more replies, while a Partners segment produces a much higher reply-to-qualified rate.

The point is methodological: measure downstream quality, not just top-of-funnel volume.
