30-second overview

What a recruiter should take from this project

This is more than dashboarding: the analysis required attribution rules, data-quality exclusions, rate normalisation and judgement about whether a result could be misleading.

Business question

Which phone-number type produces better qualified outcomes, and what happens when fresh mobile data supply declines?

Method

Matched calls to leads across campaign, sub-campaign, party and exact phone number, with a 30-minute qualification window.

Finding

Mobile/Direct/Personal calls consistently showed the stronger qualified-per-1,000-call rate.

Judgement

Challenged the apparent 4–5 pm peak after finding evidence that delayed CRM updates could bias it.

Business context

Turn call activity into an executive decision, not just a volume report.

Sales leadership needed to know whether the type of number being dialled affected qualified-lead production, and whether changes in the supply of fresh Mobile / Direct contacts were damaging performance.

I produced this type of analysis monthly in Power BI, with the CEO and directors as the main audience.

Why it mattered

The decision was about data allocation and calling behaviour.

If Mobile / Direct / Personal numbers materially outperform Switch / Landline numbers, data-building priorities and caller effort should reflect that. But a useful answer also needs to account for repeat attempts, attribution quality and the possibility that CRM behaviour can distort apparent performance.

Attribution method

Define which call actually gets credit for the Qualified outcome

The core analytical problem was matching call activity to the later CRM status change without giving credit to the wrong interaction.

1. Match identityUse campaign ID, sub-campaign ID and party ID to connect call and lead records.
2. Match exact numberRequire the actual phone number dialled to match the lead-side number evidence.
3. Classify channelGroup the dialled number as Switch/Landline or Mobile/Direct/Personal.
4. Attribute outcomeUse the call immediately before the lead became Qualified and require the update within 30 minutes.
5. Clean evidenceRemove duplicates and exclude ambiguous number matches before calculating rates.
Why the 30-minute rule matters: without a temporal boundary, a later CRM qualification could be incorrectly attributed to an older call. The rule is an operational attribution choice, not a claim that every qualification truly occurred because of that call.

Finding 2

Less fresh mobile supply led to more repeat calling, but measured conversion held up

The next question was whether callers were being hurt by receiving fewer unique fresh Mobile / Direct contacts.

What changed

The unique Mobile / Direct contact pool shrank, while call volume increased sharply. That pattern indicates more repeat attempts against a smaller pool of available contacts.

What did not deteriorate

Qualified outcomes per 1,000 Mobile / Direct calls moved from 2.92 to 3.12, so the measured conversion rate did not fall across the compared periods.

Source integrity note: the separate June–July channel analysis contains 29,772 Mobile / Direct / Personal calls, while this supply comparison contains 29,771. I preserve the two source-supplied figures rather than force them to reconcile without evidence for the one-call difference.
Finding 3 · analytical caveat

4–5 pm looked strongest — then I tested whether the result could be misleading

For 26 May to 26 July 2026, 4:00–4:59 pm recorded 3,364 calls, 21 qualified outcomes and 6.24 qualified per 1,000 calls.

The initial result

4:00–4:59 pm was the strongest recorded hour in the analysed data.

The bias check

Only 7 of the 21 qualifications were entered within five minutes of the call, or 33.3%.

That raises the possibility that some records were updated during end-of-day administration rather than immediately after the call, inflating the apparent late-afternoon result.

Recommendation: treat 4–5 pm as a hypothesis worth further testing, not as a definitive best hour. This is one of the most important parts of the case study because it shows the analysis did not stop at the most attractive-looking result.
Executive decision support

What the analysis supports operationally

The report was aimed mainly at the CEO and directors, so the useful output was the decision implication rather than a collection of charts.

Prioritise higher-value number types

Where possible, preserve or expand Mobile / Direct / Personal data supply because those calls produced stronger measured qualified rates.

Track repeat-attempt pressure

A smaller unique contact pool can drive much heavier repeat calling. Monitor unique contacts alongside call volume so activity growth is not mistaken for fresh coverage.

Audit attribution before changing schedules

Do not redesign calling hours from the 4–5 pm result alone until CRM update timing is separated from actual call-time performance.
