Attribution method

Define which interaction gets credit before comparing performance

The analytical risk was assigning a later Qualified status to the wrong call.

1. Match identityCampaign ID, sub-campaign ID and party ID connect call and lead records.
2. Match exact numberThe actual dialled phone number must match the lead-side number evidence.
3. Classify channelSwitch/Landline or Mobile/Direct/Personal.
4. Attribute outcomeUse the call immediately before Qualified and require the update within 30 minutes.
5. Clean evidenceRemove duplicates and ambiguous matches before rate calculations.
Why the 30-minute rule matters: it limits the chance of attributing a later CRM status change to an older call. It is an operational attribution rule, not proof of causality.

Finding 2

A smaller fresh mobile pool led to heavier repeat calling, but conversion did not deteriorate

The next question was whether reduced fresh-data supply was hurting caller productivity.

The smaller unique contact pool coincided with substantially more calls, indicating more repeat attempts. Qualified outcomes rose from 49 to 93 and the measured conversion rate per 1,000 calls remained broadly stable.

Source integrity: the separate June–July channel analysis contains 29,772 Mobile/Direct/Personal calls, while the supply comparison contains 29,771. Both source-supplied figures are retained because the one-call difference has not been explained.
Finding 3 · measurement bias

4–5 pm looked best, so I tested whether the result was trustworthy

For 26 May to 26 July 2026, 4:00–4:59 pm recorded 3,364 calls, 21 qualified outcomes and 6.24 qualified per 1,000 calls.

Bias check

Only 7 of the 21 qualifications were entered within five minutes of the call.

That made delayed end-of-day CRM administration a plausible source of bias in the apparent late-afternoon peak.

Recommendation

Treat 4–5 pm as a hypothesis worth further testing, not as a definitive best calling hour. Channel choice had clearer evidence than timing.

Executive decision support

What the evidence supports operationally

The useful output for the CEO and directors was the decision implication, not just the charts.

Prioritise higher-value number types

Preserve or expand Mobile / Direct / Personal data supply where feasible because those calls produced stronger measured qualified rates.

Monitor repeat-attempt pressure

Track unique contacts beside call volume so higher activity is not mistaken for wider fresh coverage.

Audit attribution before changing schedules

Separate CRM update timing from actual call-time performance before changing staffing or calling windows.

What this proves

Analysis + attribution + judgement

  • Multi-key event matching across operational systems
  • Temporal attribution logic
  • Duplicate and ambiguity exclusions
  • Rate normalisation rather than raw-volume comparison
  • Longitudinal supply-versus-activity analysis
  • Investigation of measurement bias
  • Executive interpretation and recommendations
