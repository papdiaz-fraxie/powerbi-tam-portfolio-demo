Business problem

Useful research existed, but there was no reusable repository.

Researchers manually built B2B contact and company data using LinkedIn, company websites and other online sources. Each campaign or data project had its own Google Sheet.

Before the repository, completed research remained distributed. A new list could take days or weeks to build from scratch depending on the brief.

My response

Turn one-off research into maintained reference data.

I created a BigQuery repository that retained historical research and made it searchable for client campaigns, DaaS delivery, CRM-related use, reporting and market analysis.

The design principle was simple: preserve the history, but make the latest researched build easy to use.

Operating model

Research → QA → standardisation → repository → reuse

Quality control happened before ingestion rather than treating BigQuery as a raw spreadsheet dump.

1. Client briefCampaign or DaaS requirement defines target companies and contacts.
2. Research SheetsResearchers build data in campaign-specific Google Sheets.
3. Human QASpreadsheet formulas and manual checking surface research issues.
4. Python / SQLI standardise fields and run additional validation before load.
5. BigQuery reuseHistorical builds are retained; downstream extracts use the newest suitable research.
Automation layer: I also built a Paperform → webhook → Apps Script → BigQuery workflow for form-driven data requests and exports.
Layered data quality

Human checks and programmatic checks solved different problems.

  • Country variants such as Australia / AU / AUS
  • Different phone-number formats
  • Inconsistent job-title and seniority labels
  • Missing required fields
  • Duplicate records and repeated research builds
  • Different entry conventions between campaign Sheets
Historical logic

Keep history, use the latest build.

Contacts and companies could be researched more than once. Older records remained useful for history, while new extracts normally needed the most recently researched version.

What changed operationally

A new brief no longer had to begin with “start researching.” It could begin with “what relevant, recent research already exists?”

Suitable records could be reused, the latest build selected, and research effort concentrated on missing or stale coverage.

2026 is excluded from the average because the available year was partial.
Business result

Historical research became an asset instead of a dead spreadsheet.

The value was not simply storing more rows. The repository made prior work searchable, made recency explicit and reduced unnecessary rebuilding by allowing new requests to reuse suitable researched records before commissioning fresh research.

What this proves

Data platform + data quality + operations

  • Designed a central repository around a real operational problem.
  • Combined human QA with Python/SQL controls.
  • Handled historical records and recency-aware selection.
  • Connected BigQuery to operational request workflows.
  • Worked across technical implementation and commercial data delivery.
Modern next step

Formalise observability without rewriting the story.

In a modern rebuild I would make more controls explicit through schema tests, freshness alerts, batch-quality metrics and clearer lineage from research build to downstream extract.

This is the main technical gap between the real operational platform and stronger analytics-engineering portfolio examples.
